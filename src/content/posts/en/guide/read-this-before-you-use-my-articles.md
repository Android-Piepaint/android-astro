## Introduction: Citation Is More Than Naming a Source

Articles published on personal blogs are still protected by copyright. The fact that an article is publicly accessible on the Internet does not mean that it may be copied, translated, modified, or redistributed without conditions.</br>

When referencing another person's blog post, distinguish between:</br>

- **Referencing**: discussing an author's ideas and linking to the original article without reproducing its protected expression.

- **Quoting**: reproducing a limited portion of the original text, subject to applicable quotation or copyright exceptions.

- **Reusing**: reproducing substantial portions or the entire article, translating it, adapting it, or distributing a derivative work.

- Giving credit is essential, but attribution alone does not automatically grant permission to reproduce or adapt a work.

This guide focuses on the [Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0 International License (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/deed.en).

# Understanding the Four Main Conditions

CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 has three named license elements and an additional restriction-related condition.</br>

### Attribution (BY)

You must appropriately credit the original creator and identify the source.</br>

A proper attribution should include, where available and reasonably practicable:</br>

- The author's name or pseudonym.

- The title of the original work.

A link to the original article.

- A reference to the CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license and a link to it.

- Any copyright or disclaimer notices supplied with the original work.

- An indication of whether you modified the original material.

If you translate, edit, shorten, expand, or otherwise modify the work, you must clearly disclose that fact.</br>

You must also **avoid suggesting** that the original author endorses you or your adaptation.</br>

###  Non-Commercial (NC)

You may not use the licensed material primarily for, or in a manner directly connected with, commercial advantage or monetary compensation.</br>

This does not mean that every online use is automatically non-commercial. For example, using an article in a commercial publication, paid product, advertising campaign, or revenue-oriented service may raise licensing issues.</br>

When in doubt, contact the author and obtain separate permission.</br>

### ShareAlike (SA)

When you create and share an adaptation of the original work, you must distribute your contributions under the same license elements.</br>

For example, if you translate a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 article into English and publish the translation, the adapted material must be shared under an appropriate BY-NC-SA license.</br>

The simplest approach is to use CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 again and link to the license.</br>

# How to Cite a Personal Blog Article Correctly

The appropriate citation method depends on how you use the original article.

## Referencing an Article Without Reproducing It

If you merely discuss an author's ideas and link to the original post without reproducing protected material, a conventional bibliographic reference is generally appropriate.</br>

For example:

```text
Author: EDKPiepaint
Title: “Understanding the Linux Boot Process”
Published on: EDKPiepaint's Personal Collections -- EDKPiepaint 的個人所藏
Original article: https://blog.cloudflare88.eu.org/article
```

This allows readers to locate the original source.</br>
Merely linking to an article does not, by itself, mean that your entire post must be licensed under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.

## Quoting a Portion of the Original Article

When quoting a passage:

- Clearly distinguish the quotation from your own writing.</br>
- Use quotation marks or block quotations.</br>
- Identify the author and original article.</br>
- Provide a link to the original source.</br>
- Include same license information.</br>
- Do not reproduce more material than necessary for your discussion.</br>

Some jurisdictions permit limited quotation or fair use without relying on a CC license. These exceptions vary by jurisdiction and circumstance, so a citation should not be treated as a substitute for checking the applicable legal requirements.</br>

##  Reproducing, Translating, or Adapting an Article

Before reproducing an entire article, translating it, or adapting it into another work, first verify that:</br>

- The specific article is actually licensed under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.</br>
- The license applies to the material you intend to reuse.</br>
- No third-party content is excluded from the license.</br>
- Your intended use is non-commercial under the license.</br>
- You can meet the attribution and ShareAlike requirements.</br>

You should then:</br>

- Name the original author.</br>
- State the original article's title.</br>
- Link to the original article.</br>
- Identify CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and link to its full license.</br>
- Disclose all modifications, including translation, abridgment, and additions.</br>
- Preserve relevant copyright and disclaimer notices supplied with the original.</br>
- Apply the same or a compatible license to the adapted material when sharing it.</br>

### Recommended Attribution Template

The following format can be used when translating, reproducing, or adapting a blog article:</br>

```text
Original title: “Article Title”
Original author: EDKPiepaint
Original source: EDKPiepaint's Personal Collections -- EDKPiepaint 的個人所藏
Original license: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 International
Use: Translation / Excerpt / Adaptation / Reproduction
Changes made: Translated into ...(Language), shortened selected paragraphs, and added explanatory notes.
License for this adaptation: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 International.
```

Retain any relevant copyright or disclaimer notices provided by the original author. **DO NOT** invent notices that were never supplied.

# Do and DON'Ts

## DO

- Identify the original author and source: Provide the author's name or pseudonym, the original title, and a link to the original article.</br>

- Identify the exact license: State CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 rather than simply writing “Creative Commons licensed.”</br>

- Disclose modifications.
For example: “This article is a translation of the original post, with selected paragraphs omitted and additional explanatory notes.”</br>

- Respect the ShareAlike requirement: When distributing an adaptation, apply the same or a compatible license to the adapted material.</br>

- Respect the author's reputation and independence: Do not imply that the original author endorses your work.</br>

## DON'T

- **DO NOT** write only “Source: Internet”: This does not adequately identify the original creator or work.</br>

- **DO NOT** remove the author's attribution or applicable license information: Adding a hyperlink does not justify removing required notices.</br>

- **DO NOT** present a translation or adaptation as your original work: Always disclose that the material has been modified.</br>

- **DO NOT** use NC-licensed material for an impermissible commercial purpose: Obtain separate permission when your intended use falls outside the non-commercial license grant.</br>

- **DO NOT** apply incompatible restrictions to an adaptation: For example, do not distribute a BY-NC-SA adaptation under an “All Rights Reserved” notice that prevents the reuse allowed by the applicable license.</br>

- **DO NOT** imply endorsement by the original author: Attribution is not an endorsement or sponsorship agreement.</br>

# Conclusion

Proper citation is an expression of respect for intellectual work. A good citation allows readers to identify the creator, trace the original source, understand the applicable license, and distinguish the original material from subsequent modifications.</br>

When reusing a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 work, remember three essential principles(CRS Guide):

**C**redit the creator. </br>
**R**espect the non-commercial condition. </br>
**S**hare adaptations under the same or a compatible license.</br>

Knowledge should be shared openly, but openness must go hand in hand with responsibility.
